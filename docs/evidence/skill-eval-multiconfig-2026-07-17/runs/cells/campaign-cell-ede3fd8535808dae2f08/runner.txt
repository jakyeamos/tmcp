No. One passing run is evidence, not proof: it may reflect luck, a weak bar, leakage, or overfitting to one case.

Before making a durable guidebook claim:

1. Confirm the target skill, multiple representative golden cases, and a defensible bar with clear failure smells for each case.
2. Re-run every case blindly with one fresh, context-free runner per case. Runners receive only the input—never the expected outcome or prior artifacts.
3. Have judges assess each artifact independently against its case’s bar and the skill’s first principles, citing concrete gaps rather than assigning opaque scores.
4. Inspect the live checkout after every run and remove any leaked artifacts or context.
5. Map each failure to a general skill defect, make only defensible edits, then repeat the complete blind sweep with fresh agents.
6. Report per-case pass rates, cited gaps, defect mappings, edits, and before/after re-evaluation results.

Only repeated clean sweeps across representative cases justify a carefully scoped claim. Even then, state what was tested and the limits of the evidence—not that the skill universally “works.”
