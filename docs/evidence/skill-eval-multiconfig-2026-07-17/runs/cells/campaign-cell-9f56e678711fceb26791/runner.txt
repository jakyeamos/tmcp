This is likely a bar–skill mismatch, not a demonstrated skill defect. A judgment-oriented skill should be graded on whether its artifacts make sound, defensible decisions—not whether they reproduce an exact seven-heading template. That template turns the eval into a conformance test and can create a 0% pass rate even when the underlying judgment is good.

Before editing the skill:

1. Inspect each failure and separate substantive judgment gaps from heading-only mismatches.
2. Confirm whether the skill is genuinely judgment-oriented or whether exact formatting is an actual operational requirement.
3. If judgment is the target, rewrite the bar as outcome quality plus recognizable failure smells, without prescribing the answer’s parts.
4. If the seven headings are truly mandatory, explicitly reclassify the task as conformance-oriented and defend that requirement.
5. Re-run the original skill blind against the corrected bar.

Only failures that remain after this re-evaluation should drive skill edits. Editing first would teach the skill to the faulty test rather than improve its judgment.
