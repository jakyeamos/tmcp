This indicates a likely bar-shape mismatch, not necessarily a skill defect. A judgment-oriented skill should be evaluated on whether its artifacts make sound, defensible decisions—not whether they reproduce an exact seven-heading template. That template turns the eval into a conformance test and can produce false failures even when the underlying judgment is good.

Before editing the skill:

1. Verify that the skill is genuinely judgment-oriented rather than intended to satisfy a fixed output contract.
2. Inspect the cited failures: if they concern only heading count or names while the artifacts meet the substantive standard, classify the defect as belonging to the golden-case bar.
3. Rewrite the bar around the outcome a good artifact must achieve and the smells that would make it bad, without prescribing the answer’s structure.
4. Echo the revised bar back for agreement.
5. Re-run each case blind with fresh runners and judge the new artifacts against that revised bar.

Only failures that remain under a defensible, judgment-shaped bar should drive edits to the skill. Do not alter the skill merely to teach it the seven-heading test.
